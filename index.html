01

How I work

The same four steps, whether the question is about a political party's Facebook page or an Iron Age hillfort.

Collect

Pull data from public archives and open APIs, at scale and by the book.

Wikidata · Wikipedia · OpenStreetMap · Ordnance Survey · CrowdTangle archives · Meta Ad Library

Link

Match records that were never meant to meet: a fort to its article, a parish to its Domesday entry.

Fuzzy matching · geocoding · entity resolution

Model

Ask a sharp question and test it properly, with the caveats left in.

Regression · concentration and inequality · spatial analysis · time series

Show

Build something people can explore themselves, then write it up for everyone else.

Interactive maps · charts · single-file web apps · preprints

03

Platforms and politics

My main research field: how parties, leaders and campaigns use social media, and what it does for them.

  • 01

    Janus-faced campaigning

    Parties show different faces on different channels. My PhD followed what they say on Facebook against everything else they do.

  • 02

    Engagement across Europe

    Which parties, leaders and posts win attention on Facebook, from populists and new parties to Covid and election nights.

  • 03

    Satellite campaigns

    Groups such as Momentum that campaign alongside a party, and what they add to it.

  • 04

    Ads and targeting

    Paid and earned reach, and how British parties targeted voters at the 2017 and 2019 general elections.

  • 05

    Comment sections

    Who speaks, how hostile it gets and what candidates' own posts do to the tone.
